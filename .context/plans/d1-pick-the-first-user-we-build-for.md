### D1 — Pick the first user we build for

Reply with **A, B, or C**.

ELI10: “Private insurers” is a company, not a user. The demo needs one person with a concrete job, a concrete painful queue, and a concrete decision to make. That choice determines the input data, the evidence shown, and the moat story.

Recommendation: **A** because it creates the sharpest 24-hour workflow: ingest one suspicious claim, detect the issue, cite the evidence, and produce a review-ready recovery recommendation.

A) **Payment-integrity / claims analyst** *(recommended)*. Note: options differ in kind, not coverage — no completeness score. They manually investigate questionable bills before or after payment and need defensible evidence, not another generic fraud score. The demo becomes: “Here are the three bills worth reviewing first, why, and the dollar exposure.”

B) **VP of claims or medical-cost leader**. Note: options differ in kind, not coverage — no completeness score. They own MLR and buy the outcome, but they do not personally investigate claims. The demo emphasizes recovered dollars and trends, which is judge-friendly but weaker as a credible daily workflow.

C) **Network / provider-contracting leader**. Note: options differ in kind, not coverage — no completeness score. They care that providers bill outside contract terms or submit mismatched claims. This ties to the old provider-validation concept, but makes the product more about contract enforcement than AI-generated or unsupported bills.

Net: A gives us a believable user workflow and lets B remain the buyer story.
