# Ghost Network Fraud Detection Platform

## Problem

Health insurance companies must maintain accurate provider directories — lists of in-network doctors and hospitals. These directories are routinely inflated with non-existent, retired, or unavailable providers. This is the "ghost network" problem.

**Consequences:**
- Members can't access care they were promised when they bought the plan
- Insurers fraudulently inflate network adequacy metrics to claim public funding (Medicaid, Medicare Advantage)
- CMS requires quarterly network adequacy audits; most state Medicaid agencies and managed care organizations lack automated tooling to run them

The fraud is structural: insurers benefit from appearing to have broad networks, and the auditing infrastructure is too slow and manual to catch it at scale.

## Solution

An automated AI platform combining two layers:

1. **Registry cross-reference engine** — ingests NPI registry, NPPES, state licensing boards, CMS participation files, and compares them algorithmically against insurer-submitted provider directories to flag high-probability ghost entries

2. **AI voice verification agents** — outbound agents that simulate appointment booking calls (plan-specific, member-specific) to confirm provider availability and actual network participation, producing legally defensible verification records

The two layers work in sequence: data cross-referencing triages the full directory down to high-risk entries; voice agents confirm the flagged cases. This keeps call volume manageable while maximizing accuracy.

## Critical Technical Constraint

Generic calls ("are you in network?") are not sufficient verification — receptionists don't reliably know, and network status is plan-specific. The verification standard that holds up to regulatory scrutiny requires simulating an actual appointment booking request for a **specific plan + member combination**. Voice agents must operate at this level of specificity, not generic inquiry. This is the core technical differentiator.

## Target Markets

### Phase 1 — Public Sector (Fraud Detection)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Buyer** | State Medicaid agencies, CMS/OIG offices |
| **Problem they have** | Mandated quarterly audits with no automated tooling; manual sampling misses systemic fraud |
| **Value delivered** | Automated audit at scale → recoupment actions against fraudulent insurers → recovered public funds |
| **Revenue model** | CMS Innovation grants, OIG grants, state contracts |
| **Why start here** | Regulatory use generates published methodology and enforcement track record — the foundation for enterprise sales |

### Phase 2 — Enterprise Compliance (Insurance Companies)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Buyer** | Health insurers operating Medicare Advantage and Medicaid managed care plans |
| **Framing** | "Survive the CMS audit before it happens" — not "find your fraud" |
| **Problem they have** | Upcoming or ongoing CMS network adequacy audits, with clawback and penalty risk |
| **Value delivered** | Identify and remediate directory gaps before enforcement; audit-ready reports on demand |
| **Revenue model** | SaaS licensing, per-verification pricing |
| **Why this framing works** | Insurers won't pay to surface their own liability; they will pay to reduce enforcement exposure |

## Moat

Not the scraping layer — that's commoditized (NPPES, NPI, CMS files are public). The defensible moat is:

1. **Verification methodology** — appointment simulation at plan+member specificity
2. **Regulatory acceptance** — methodology validated through public sector pilots and published CMS-aligned output format
3. **Adjudication logic** — classification system that distinguishes fraud from data quality issues, with evidence chains that hold up to legal scrutiny

## Go-to-Market Sequencing

```
Public sector pilots (CMS/Medicaid)
        ↓
Published methodology + enforcement track record
        ↓
Enterprise sales to insurers (defensive compliance)
```

The bridge from Phase 1 to Phase 2 is concrete: regulatory use generates the published methodology and case studies that give insurers confidence in the tool's regulatory defensibility. This is the asset that unlocks enterprise sales.

| Moat definition | Verification methodology + regulatory acceptance, not scraping | Focus R&D investment correctly |

## Hackathon Demo Targets

- **Working demo**: AI voice agent calling a mock provider office with plan+member context, returning a structured verification result
- **Dashboard**: Ghost network detection rate by geography and insurer, with confidence scores
- **Sample audit report**: CMS-compliant output format showing evidence chain for verified and flagged entries
- **Data layer**: Discrepancy detection against a sample NPI/NPPES snapshot vs. a synthetic insurer directory

## Competitive Landscape

| Competitor | What they do | Gap we fill |
|---|---|---|
| Manual auditing (status quo) | State auditors sample ~5% of directories | We audit 100% at quarterly cadence |
| Symplr / Availity / CAQH | Directory data normalization and provider credentialing | Not focused on fraud detection or regulatory enforcement output |
| General healthcare AI | Broad clinical and ops AI | No specialization in network adequacy fraud |
