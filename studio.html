    DNHACKS · CLAIM INTEGRITY

    Two questions,
    kept separate.

    How much of this claim's own content needs a person to look at it — and how well does the provider record match the federal registry? Answering them together is how a coding error turns into an accusation. So we don't.

    AXIS ONE

    The claim

    Deterministic rules over the line items. Instant, repeatable, no model involved.

    AXIS TWO

    The provider record

    An agent panel against the federal registry. Slow, fallible, and never allowed to block intake.

    Checking what is live…

    SCENE TWO

    Pick something to run

    Nine fixtures. Every NPI begins with 9 — a range CMS has never assigned — so no real provider can be implicated. Each one states what it is supposed to prove before it runs, which is the only way a demo can be wrong in public.

    SCENE THREE

    Running both axes

    The claim rules finish immediately. The provider panel is talking to the federal registry, so it takes as long as it takes — there is no progress to report until it answers.

      SCENE FIVE

      What it actually saw

      Every observation carries the evidence behind it and, where one exists, the ordinary explanation. A finding you cannot argue with is not a finding.

      SCENE SIX

      Everything assessed

      Both axes side by side, per claim. Where a provider band is missing, the check did not complete — that is a gap in our coverage, not a finding about the provider.
