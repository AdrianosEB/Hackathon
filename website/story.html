    DNHACKS · TWO-AXIS CLAIM REVIEW

    TWO AXES.
    ONE CLAIM.

    A CODING ERROR IS NOT AN ACCUSATION.

    How much of this claim's own content needs a person to look at it, and how well the provider record matches the federal registry, are different questions. Answering them together is how one becomes the other. So we keep them apart.

    SCROLL THE STORY

    THE METHOD

    TWO QUESTIONS.
    NOT ONE SCORE.

    “Axis” just means a separate measurement. One measures what needs review in this bill; the other measures how complete and consistent the provider record is. They do not add together, because a billing issue is not evidence about a provider.

    01

    THIS BILL

    Deterministic rules over the line items — repeated codes, units out of band, prices that do not match — and then a second, separate pass where a model reads the note against what was billed. This answers: how much should a person review this claim? Every finding says which of the two found it.

    OUTPUT · REVIEW PRIORITY 0–100 · LOW / REVIEW / HIGH

    02

    THE PROVIDER RECORD

    An agent panel queries the federal registry and reports what corroborates and what does not. This answers: how much evidence do we have that this record is complete and consistent? It is slow, fallible, and never allowed to block intake. A low score is a statement about the evidence, never about the provider.

    OUTPUT · DATA CONFIDENCE 0–100 · CONFIRMED / PROBABLE / INCOMPLETE / CONFLICTING

    Where our own coverage is incomplete, the interface says so in the same colour it uses for itself — never in a warning colour. A gap on our side must not read as a mark against a provider. And when a provider disagrees with a finding, that is not the end of the process: it is the start of scene seven.
